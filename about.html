How Access came together

A school built where technique meets the working frame.

Access Acting Studio was formed in response to the low standard of acting too often accepted in modern film and television.

Actors are required to be truthful, present, connected, natural, responsive to direction, and alive on camera. Too often the result is weaker: performances that are emotionally vague, technically unprepared, disconnected from the other actor, unaware of the frame, or unable to stay truthful under pressure.

That gap does not arise because actors lack feeling or ambition. It arises because the words actors are given — be natural, be present, be truthful — can remain ideals instead of becoming tools. Screen work requires the actor to convert inner preparation into visible behaviour while holding thought, relationship, objective, continuity, camera awareness, and adjustment all at once.

Robert Rosina was looking at this through film, story, technique, and performance construction: what reaches the lens, how preparation becomes behaviour, how choices shape the scene, and how performance affects the audience.

David Barry was looking at the same gap from inside professional screen work: preparation, listening, continuity, adjustment under pressure, and the discipline of staying truthful inside the frame.

Access Acting Studio was formed where those two perspectives met.

The school brings together method-led preparation, practical scene work, camera practice, playback review, and professional insight. It was built for actors who want a clear process, playable tools, visible progress, and a stronger pathway from training into serious screen work.

Founders

The people behind Access.

Access is led by founders who bring screen performance, acting technique, production thinking, and story service into the training room.

Founder

David Barry

David Barry is a screen actor and teacher whose work at Access supports practical actor training, camera awareness, scene preparation, and disciplined rehearsal.

He brings professional experience into the room through direct feedback, structured exercises, and a clear focus on what actors need when working on camera.

His teaching helps actors listen, adjust, repeat, and make choices that remain alive within the frame.

Founder

Robert Rosina

Robert Rosina brings film production training, extensive acting technique study, professional strategic communication, and production-led storytelling to Access.

His teaching focus is practical: scene analysis, behaviour, audience effect, camera evidence, and a repeatable method actors can use under pressure.

Robert’s core principle for the school is simple: actors learn to access truthful behaviour and shape it with purpose in service of the story.
